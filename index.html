Project Lead · INEN 6301 UX with AI · 2026

Check-Mate

A mobile app that suggests a short break when your fatigue checks say you need one, then measures whether the break helped.

Adaptive micro-breaksReaction-time testMobile web appUser testing
Open the app Step-by-step walkthrough
Video demo coming soonA two-minute tour of the app from sign-up to break history.
Read the case study

The problem

Fixed break schedules ignore how tired a person actually is. People either break too late, after errors creep in, or break when they don't need to and lose time.

How it works

  • Set up. Create an account and pick favourite break activities
  • Quick check. A sleepiness rating plus a reaction-time test
  • Break. One short activity of up to 10 minutes, matched to fatigue risk
  • Recover. Rate how you feel and retest, so recovery is measured

My role

  • Led the team through the literature review and the study methodology
  • Designed and built the user interface with teammate Nirman
  • Leading the final stages of development and preparing the user experiments

Why it matters

It turns microbreak research into something a worker can use on their phone. Every check and break is logged and can be exported as CSV, so the data feeds straight into analysis.

TaktArm Twin

The problem

Programming a cobot for each new task is slow, and operators often cannot see what the robot will do before it moves.

What I built

  • Plain-language commands read by an offline language model
  • A preview arm that shows the plan before anything moves
  • The fastest process within the 1000 mm/s safety limit, with the time it saves
  • Charts comparing cycle time, energy and peak power across speeds
  • Payload checks against the 12.5 kg limit and bin fit checks

Results in simulation

37 to 40%
shorter predicted cycle time
65.8 s → 39.5 s
six-object batch
≈ ⅓
less predicted energy per cycle

These are twin predictions. They will be checked against measurements on the real robot.

Human factors angle

The operator stays in control, because no motion runs without approval. A planned user study will measure workload (NASA-TLX), trust and usability (SUS).

Built with

Python, FastAPI, Qwen 2.5 3B through Ollama, Three.js with the official UR10e model, NumPy and OpenCV. About 9,700 lines of code and 83 automated tests. Hardware is a UR10e with a Robotiq Hand-E gripper and wrist camera.

03 · Research

Research

Human Factors and Ergonomics Laboratory, Lamar University, advised by Dr. Yueqing Li.

Current doctoral research

AI-adaptive microbreak scheduling based on fatigue modelling

Long work sessions build up physical and mental fatigue, and that is when errors and strain injuries happen. I am using wearable data, such as heart rate variability and stress scores from Garmin watches, with machine learning to estimate fatigue and schedule short breaks before performance drops.

The goal is simple. Detect the early signs, step in early, and keep people safe without slowing the work down.
